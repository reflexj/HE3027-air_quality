NULL RESULTS: WHAT WE TESTED THAT HAD NO (ROBUST) EFFECT
==========================================================

A running list of everything we tested that showed no effect, or no robust one.
Add a new entry every time a test comes back empty.
Numbers are copied from the .md file named in "Source"; rerun that script to reproduce them.
The file name says which model the test was run on (model_v1* = v1, model_v2 = v2).

PM10 effect = % change in price for +1 ug/m3 PM10.
"pp" = percentage points of the PM10 effect (for interactions).
Last updated: 2026-10-23


1. NO EFFECT ON PRICES
----------------------

PM10 squared (non-linear PM10 effect)
  Test:    PM10 + PM10^2 in the v2 model (3-year average PM10).
  Result:  +0.008 pp per +1 ug/m3 of PM10 level, p = 0.263. Not significant in
           any specification (joint model, yearly PM10, borough-by-year FE,
           borough clusters). Effect -0.49% at P10 of PM10 vs -0.40% at P90:
           if anything slightly smaller at high levels, not larger.
  Verdict: No non-linearity. A linear PM10 term is enough.
  Source:  model_v2.md

Income level: WTP by income group
  Test:    PM10 effect and WTP by low / middle / high income, with three
           income measures (LSOA household income, MSOA household income,
           borough pay), plus a continuous PM10 x ln(income) term.
  Result:  Preferred measure (LSOA income): WTP GBP 640 / 580 / 640 per ug/m3,
           joint test of equal WTP p = 0.977; equal % effects p = 0.293.
           PM10 x ln(LSOA income) on its own: +0.037 (s.e. 0.125), p = 0.766.
           Borough pay groups: no significant difference in separate
           regressions (p = 0.196 MSOA / 0.459 borough clusters).
  Verdict: No visible income gradient (but the confidence intervals are wide,
           so a moderate gradient cannot be ruled out).
  Caveat:  In the v2 joint model, together with PM10 x no qualifications,
           PM10 x ln(LSOA income) is -0.580 pp (p = 0.022). The two are
           correlated (-0.59), so this only appears conditional on education.
  Source:  model_v1_income_groups.md, model_v1_income_small_area.md, model_v2.md

Share of vulnerable residents (aged 0-15 or 65+)
  Test:    PM10 x share aged 0-15 or 65+ in the LSOA (2011), per +10 pp.
  Result:  -0.080 pp, p = 0.407. Sign flips with yearly PM10 (+0.098, p = 0.078).
           Split: children +0.091 (p = 0.439), over-65s -0.266 (p = 0.101).
  Verdict: No robust effect. Over-65s come closest.
  Source:  model_v2.md

Education measured as share with a degree
  Test:    PM10 x share with Level 4+ qualifications, per +10 pp.
  Result:  +0.013 pp, p = 0.799.
  Verdict: No effect. (See "weak results" below for the no-qualifications share.)
  Source:  model_v2.md

NOx as a second pollutant
  Test:    Add NOx to the v1 model (M8).
  Result:  NOx coefficient +0.00% (s.e. 0.01); PM10 effect unchanged at -0.21%.
  Verdict: NOx has no effect once PM10 is in; PM10 is not proxying for NOx.
  Source:  model_v1.md

Crime
  Test:    Add log(1 + crimes in the LSOA-year) to the v1 model (M8), 2010-2014 only.
  Result:  Crime coefficient -0.0039 (s.e. 0.0053), not significant;
           PM10 effect -0.11% with and without crime.
  Verdict: Crime has no effect within LSOAs and does not explain the PM10 effect.
  Source:  model_v1.md

PM10 x near a main road
  Test:    PM10 x within 100 m of a motorway or A road (OS Open Roads), in the
           v2 model with road-distance and noise bands as controls.
  Result:  -0.010 pp, p = 0.923. Not significant in any check (yearly PM10,
           borough-by-year FE, LSOA-by-year FE, borough clusters).
  Verdict: The PM10 discount is no larger right next to main roads.
  Source:  model_v2_roads.md

PM10 x road noise
  Test:    PM10 x noise of 65 dB or more (Defra road noise map 2012, Lden).
  Result:  -0.182 pp, p = 0.319. Not significant in any check.
  Verdict: The PM10 discount is no larger in noisy places.
  Source:  model_v2_roads.md

Main roads as the reason for the larger flat discount
  Test:    PM10 effect for flats / houses near (<100 m) and far from a main
           road; PM10 x house with and without road and noise controls.
  Result:  Flats near -0.47%, flats far -0.51%; houses near -0.11%, houses
           far -0.13%. Difference-in-differences +0.009 pp, p = 0.768.
           PM10 x house +0.346 -> +0.367 pp with road and noise controls.
  Verdict: Flats are more often near main roads (38% vs 15%), but that is NOT
           why their PM10 discount is larger. Floor level is the next test.
  Source:  model_v2_roads.md

Main roads beyond 50 m, and road noise below 65 dB
  Test:    Distance bands 50-100, 100-200, 200-400 m (base 400 m+) and noise
           bands 55-59.9 and 60-64.9 dB (base below 55 dB), within LSOAs.
  Result:  All close to zero and not significant (largest: 100-200 m +1.1%).
  Verdict: Only homes right on a main road (0-50 m: -2.0% to -2.5%) and loud
           places (65-69.9 dB: about -2.3%) are discounted. No gradient beyond.
  Source:  model_v2_roads.md

PM10 x energy efficiency (EPC rating)
  Test:    PM10 x average EPC score of the homes of the same type (flat /
           house) in the postcode, per +10 points (about one band), in the v1
           model (M8) + PM10 x house + PM10 x new build. Hypothesis: efficient
           homes are less affected (positive interaction).
  Result:  -0.028 pp (s.e. 0.065), p = 0.668; 95% CI [-0.156, +0.100].
           Never significantly positive in 11 checks (efficiency-specific year
           effects, borough-by-year FE, LSOA-by-year FE, borough clusters,
           3-year average PM10, all homes in the postcode, 10+ certificates,
           flats only, houses only).
  Verdict: No support for the hypothesis. One band more efficiency removes at
           most 47% of the PM10 discount (upper end of the CI). Caveat: postcode
           average, not the home itself, and ratings from 2008-today.
  Source:  model_v1_energy_efficiency.md

PM10 x local park within 400 m
  Test:    PM10 x public park or garden of 2 ha+ within 400 m of the postcode
           (London Plan standard; OS Open Greenspace), v1 model (M8) + PM10 x
           house + PM10 x new build.
  Result:  +0.018 pp, p = 0.773. Not significant in any of 11 checks.
  Verdict: Having a local park nearby does not change the PM10 discount.
  Source:  model_v1_green_space.md


2. TESTED, BUT DID NOT CHANGE THE PM10 ESTIMATE
------------------------------------------------
(These may fit the data better, but they leave the PM10 effect where it was.)

Non-linear distance controls (in the LSOA fixed-effects model)
  Test:    Seven functional forms for the five distances (quadratic, log,
           log-quadratic, one or all distances).
  Result:  PM10 effect between -0.20% and -0.22% in all of them (linear: -0.21%).
           R^2 gain at most +0.0022. They do matter in models WITHOUT LSOA FE.
  Verdict: No change with LSOA FE. Kept as log-quadratic in v2 for fit.
  Source:  model_v1_non-linear.md

Functional form of income (borough pay)
  Test:    No income, linear, log, quadratic, cubic pay.
  Result:  PM10 effect -0.20% to -0.21% in every form.
  Verdict: No change. Cubic kept in v2 for fit.
  Source:  model_v1_income_groups.md

Dropping the 2011-2012 PM10 jump
  Test:    v1 model (M8) without 2011, and without 2011 and 2012.
  Result:  -0.21% and -0.25% (vs -0.21%).
  Verdict: Little change; year fixed effects absorb most of the jump.
  Source:  model_v1_pm10_smoothing.md

Last year's PM10 instead of this year's
  Test:    PM10 of year t-1 in the v1 model (M8).
  Result:  -0.20% (vs -0.20% for yearly PM10 on the same sample).
  Verdict: No change. (Smoothing over 2-3 years DOES change it: -0.37%.)
  Source:  model_v1_pm10_smoothing.md

Distance to main roads and road noise as controls
  Test:    Add distance bands to the nearest motorway / A road and road noise
           bands to the v2 model.
  Result:  PM10 effect -0.36% -> -0.36%. Roads and noise do lower prices
           themselves (both jointly significant).
  Verdict: No change. They capture local exposure the monitoring sites miss,
           while PM10 is identified from changes over time.
  Source:  model_v2_roads.md


3. WEAK OR FRAGILE RESULTS (NOT ROBUST ENOUGH TO CLAIM)
-------------------------------------------------------

Education measured as share without qualifications
  Test:    PM10 x share without qualifications, per +10 pp.
  Result:  -0.150 pp, p = 0.047 (larger discount in less-educated areas).
           Not significant with borough-by-year FE (p = 0.102), with yearly
           PM10 (p = 0.301), or after the Bonferroni correction for four
           interactions (needs p < 0.0125).
  Verdict: Weak hint only. Opposite sign to the "informed buyers" idea.
  Source:  model_v2.md

Efficient postcodes (EPC average C or better) and the PM10 effect
  Test:    PM10 x efficient (A-C) vs average (D) postcode, v1 model (M8)
           + PM10 x house + PM10 x new build.
  Result:  -0.184 pp, p = 0.068 (LARGER discount in efficient postcodes:
           -0.35% vs -0.17%). Significant at 5% in 4 of 11 checks, but not
           with borough clusters, 3-year average PM10, 10+ certificates per
           postcode (+0.063), flats only or houses only.
  Verdict: Weak hint only, opposite to the hypothesis. Efficient postcodes are
           mostly new-build flats (87% flats, 30% new builds), so it may be
           about these developments, not about efficiency.
  Source:  model_v1_energy_efficiency.md

Green space around the home and the PM10 effect
  Test:    PM10 x share of green space within 500 m of the postcode (OS Open
           Greenspace), per +10 pp, and by terciles of that share, v1 model
           (M8) + PM10 x house + PM10 x new build. Two-sided (buffer vs
           exposure idea).
  Result:  Continuous: +0.080 pp, p = 0.062 (smaller discount where greener).
           Terciles: PM10 effect -0.31% (least green third, < 4% green) vs
           -0.15% / -0.13% (middle / greenest); high - low +0.179 pp,
           p = 0.013 (passes Bonferroni for 3 measures). Significant at 5% in
           10 of 14 checks (also with road / noise and distance-to-centre
           controls), but NOT with LSOA-by-year FE (+0.164, p > 0.1), with a
           250 m radius (0.000) or with public parks only (+0.075).
  Verdict: Weak evidence for the buffer idea: homes with almost no green
           space nearby carry a larger PM10 discount. Not robust enough to
           claim; the local-park measure shows nothing.
  Source:  model_v1_green_space.md

PM10 main effect with borough-by-year fixed effects
  Test:    v1 model (M8) + borough-by-year FE.
  Result:  -0.07%, not significant (vs -0.21%).
  Verdict: Either borough-wide shocks drive part of the PM10 effect, or these FE
           absorb most of the PM10 variation. Cannot tell apart.
  Source:  model_v1.md
